Hide sexual activity in MiniCycle: biometric unlock explained

Published June 11, 2026·by Chanwook Kim, MiniCycle developer·Updated November 24, 2026·3 min read

At a glance

MiniCycle lets you hide sexual-activity marks from the calendar without deleting the records. Showing them again requires successful authentication with the device’s supported, enrolled Face ID or Touch ID. The hide setting belongs to each device: check it on another iPhone before enabling iCloud Sync there.

A purple dot, and one tap to make it

Logging is deliberately small. Tap a date on the calendar and the day panel below shows a heart-shaped button for sexual activity. Tap it once and a small purple dot appears under that date, next to whatever else the day holds. Tap it again and the dot is gone. There is no separate diary, no detail form, no rating. One mark per day, on or off.

The dot sits on the calendar for a practical reason: context. Sexual activity viewed alongside period dates and the estimated fertile window is useful to have in one place, whether you are trying to conceive or trying not to. The legend lists it in purple, next to the pink of period days and the sky blue of the fertile window.

What the hide switch changes

Open Settings and turn on the option to hide sexual activity. The purple dots disappear from the calendar, the item leaves the legend, and the heart button is removed from the selected-day panel. The setting itself remains visible in Settings, so this is a screen-privacy feature rather than a way to conceal that MiniCycle supports this kind of record.

The app does not ask for authentication when you hide the records. They disappear from the calendar as soon as the switch is turned on.

How Face ID or Touch ID shows hidden records

To show hidden records, MiniCycle asks iOS for biometric authentication. Apple’s biometric authentication policy uses Face ID or Touch ID according to the device’s available, enrolled biometrics. The app checks availability first and changes the hide setting only after authentication succeeds. If authentication fails or you cancel, the records stay hidden.

MiniCycle receives the authentication result, not your face or fingerprint data. This protects the in-app visibility switch; it does not replace the iPhone passcode or other device-security settings.

Hiding is not deleting

Hiding does not erase entries. After successful biometric authentication, MiniCycle can display the records again. Hiding a record and deleting it are separate actions.

The flip side is also true. While hidden, the logging button is gone as well, so you cannot add or remove entries until you unhide. If you want a record actually gone, that is a different action. Tap the date and tap the heart button off to clear a single day, or use the data reset in Settings, which permanently deletes saved period ranges and daily logs, including sexual-activity entries.

For the separate action of removing all app records, see How to reset MiniCycle and erase all your period records.

The limits, stated plainly

Touch ID is not categorically excluded. What matters is whether iOS can authenticate with the biometrics supported and enrolled on that device. If biometrics are unavailable, have not been set up, or are temporarily locked after failed attempts, MiniCycle cannot unhide the records through this route. Check the device’s biometric settings and follow any iOS instructions before trying again.

MiniCycle does not provide an in-app passcode alternative for this switch. iOS may require the device passcode to re-enable biometrics after a lockout; that is separate from using a passcode to unhide records inside MiniCycle. Do not assume that entering a passcode in the app will reveal them.

Be clear about what this protects against, too. It guards against eyes on your open calendar: a borrowed phone, a shared photo moment, a screen visible on a desk. It is not device security; that is your passcode's job. One more detail helps here. The home-screen widget never displays sexual activity at all, hidden or not, so the most public surface of the app was never part of the problem.

Where the records live

Sexual activity is stored with your other MiniCycle records on the device by default. Optional iCloud Sync can copy those records through Apple CloudKit to another iPhone using the same Apple account. It does not apply this device’s hide setting to the other phone. See the MiniCycle privacy policy for the distinction between records and device settings.

Hiding is off by default when there is no saved setting. If keeping the marks hidden on another iPhone matters, open MiniCycle Settings on that phone and check that hiding is on before enabling iCloud Sync there. Do not assume that a hide setting from your first phone will follow the records. Check again after a restore or device transfer, which is separate from MiniCycle’s record sync.

The hide switch changes only what MiniCycle shows on screen. It does not encrypt, delete, or move the records. The estimated fertile-window and ovulation marks beside them are planning references, not medical advice, contraception, or a pregnancy test.
