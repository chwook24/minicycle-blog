Using MiniCycle's fertile window when trying to conceive

Published June 28, 2026·by Chanwook Kim, MiniCycle developer·Updated September 2, 2026·3 min read

At a glance

MiniCycle estimates an ovulation date from recorded period starts and highlights the five days before it through the following day. ACOG describes pregnancy as possible across the same relative range around actual ovulation. The important difference is that MiniCycle does not know the actual ovulation date; its seven highlighted dates are a calendar estimate, not a measurement of fertility.

Read the seven highlighted dates as an estimate

The sky-blue band starts five days before MiniCycle's estimated ovulation date and ends one day after it, for seven calendar dates in all. ACOG says pregnancy can occur from five days before actual ovulation through one day afterward.

The ranges use the same offsets, but MiniCycle's center date is only an estimate from past period starts. The blue band cannot confirm fertility inside it or rule out pregnancy outside it.

Plan across several days, not one predicted date

Clinical guidance commonly recommends regular intercourse across the fertile window rather than trying to identify one perfect day. ACOG suggests every day or every other day during that time. The days before actual ovulation matter because sperm may already be present when an egg is released.

The limitation is that MiniCycle does not know the actual ovulation day. Use the highlighted dates as a broad prompt, not as proof that the most fertile days have been found.

Log intimacy and check it against the window

MiniCycle can record sexual activity as a small purple mark on a date, tapped on or off from the day panel. For someone tracking a cycle in general, this is just context. When you are trying to conceive, it turns into feedback. Logged next to the sky-blue band, the purple marks show at a glance whether the days you had sex actually fell inside the fertile window or drifted to its edges.

A month where the marks sit outside the window is not a failure. It is information for the next one. The two sets of marks live on the same calendar precisely so you can see them together, without doing the counting in your head.

Use notes without treating signs as confirmation

Cervical mucus and basal body temperature can be used as parts of a fertility-awareness method, but neither a calendar highlight nor one observation proves the exact ovulation time. A sustained temperature rise mainly supports that ovulation may already have occurred.

MiniCycle's daily note can store what you observed on a date. If you want to use fertility-awareness methods to achieve or avoid pregnancy, follow a validated method with appropriate clinical instruction rather than inventing rules from the app's color band.

For what each observation can and cannot show, read Ovulation signs and basal body temperature: the basics.

Set an estimate-based reminder

MiniCycle can schedule a local reminder for the estimated fertile-window start or estimated ovulation day, on the date itself or up to three days earlier. When a new period record changes the estimate, the app reschedules those notifications.

The notification is still based on a calendar estimate. It does not mean the phone detected a biological change.

Where the estimate stops, and when to ask for help

An app cannot confirm ovulation or pregnancy. If your periods are very irregular, date-based estimates become less useful, and a clinician can help choose a more appropriate approach.

ACOG and Mayo Clinic suggest a fertility evaluation after one year of trying for people under 35, after six months for people 35 or older, and sooner when there is a known condition that may affect fertility. MiniCycle records are stored on the device by default; optional iCloud Sync stores a copy in your CloudKit private database. Neither storage choice changes the medical limits of the estimate.
